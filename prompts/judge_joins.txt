We are working on text-to-SQL over a data lake. A user asks a question in natural
language, and we have found the tables that can answer it. Some pairs of these tables
can be joined in more than one way, and each way connects different rows.

For each pair of tables below, keep the way or ways of joining them that make sense
for this question. If more than one way makes sense, keep each one of them, since the
question can have more than one valid meaning. If none does, keep none.

Example 1
Question: Which employees work in the Sales department?
Join 1: hr.employee (one row per employee) and hr.department (one row per department)
  1. hr.employee.dept_id = hr.department.dept_id (the department the employee works in)
  2. hr.employee.emp_id = hr.department.manager_id (the employee who manages the department)
Answer: {"1": [1]}
Why: "work in" is the department the employee belongs to. The second way connects a
department to its manager, which is not what the question is asking for.

Example 2
Question: Which flights were at Lyon airport on 3 May?
Join 1: air.flight (one row per flight) and air.airport (one row per airport)
  1. air.flight.from_airport = air.airport.code (the airport the flight leaves from)
  2. air.flight.to_airport = air.airport.code (the airport the flight lands at)
Answer: {"1": [1, 2]}
Why: "at Lyon" can mean leaving Lyon or landing at Lyon. Both make sense.

Example 3
Question: Which teams won a match in 2024?
Join 1: league.match (one row per match) and league.team (one row per team)
  1. league.match.home_team = league.team.team_id (the team that played at home)
  2. league.match.away_team = league.team.team_id (the team that played away)
  3. league.match.winner = league.team.team_id (the team that won the match)
Answer: {"1": [3]}
Why: the question asks about winning, so only the winner makes sense. Playing at home
or away does not mean the team won.

Output Format, JSON only:
{"<join number>": [<numbers of the ways that make sense>, ...]}

Return an answer only in this exact Output Format.
