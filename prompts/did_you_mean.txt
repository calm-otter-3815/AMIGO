A person asked a question about a data lake. One piece of what they asked fits
nothing in the tables kept for the question, so code has gathered, from the whole
lake, the tables and columns that sit nearest to it: first the columns that store its
value and the tables its value names, then the ones nearest in meaning. Your job is
to say which of those a person could plausibly have meant by that piece, and to word
the short question that asks them which one.

Each candidate line is: the path | what it holds. A table's line starts with "table"
and its row count. A line that ends "(outside the tables kept so far)" is in the lake
but was not among the tables kept for this question.

How to choose:

- Keep at most four candidates, nearest meaning first. Fewer is better than four
  weak ones.
- A table fits when each of its rows is the thing the piece names. A column fits when
  it names the piece or stores its value. A line that says the column stores the
  piece's value is evidence from the data: keep it unless it clearly means something
  else.
- Keep a candidate only if a person writing that piece could have meant it. A path
  that merely shares a word with the piece is not a match.
- If nothing on the list could be what they meant, return an empty list of
  options. Inventing a near miss sends them down the wrong path.
- Copy each path exactly as it is printed below, without the word table. Never name
  a path that is not on the list, and never name one twice.

How to word it:

- The question is one short sentence in plain words, about what the person meant,
  never about columns, tables or interpretations. No jargon, no SQL.
- Each label is at most 60 characters, and says what that choice means in the
  person's terms, not the path's name. Two labels are never the same.

Output Format, JSON and nothing else:

{
  "question": "one short sentence",
  "options": [
    {"column": "the path exactly as printed", "label": "at most 60 characters"}
  ]
}

Example 1
Question: Which wines from the 2018 vintage scored above 90?
The piece no kept table holds: wine's grape
Candidates:
  cellar.wine.variety | the grape variety the wine is pressed from
  cellar.wine.blend_note | free text describing how a blended wine was put together
  cellar.wine.region | the growing region printed on the label
  cellar.producer.name | the estate that made the wine

{
  "question": "Do you mean the kind of grape, or how the wine was blended?",
  "options": [
    {"column": "cellar.wine.variety", "label": "the kind of grape"},
    {"column": "cellar.wine.blend_note", "label": "how the wine was blended"}
  ]
}

Example 2
Question: Which recipes are the hardest to cook and take the longest?
The piece no kept table holds: recipe's difficulty
Candidates:
  kitchen.recipe.prep_minutes | how long the ingredients take to prepare
  kitchen.recipe.servings | how many people the recipe feeds
  kitchen.recipe.calories | energy per serving

{
  "question": "Nothing here records how hard a recipe is. Should we drop that part?",
  "options": []
}

Example 3
Question: Which customers sent a parcel by express last month?
The piece no kept table holds: shipping speed = "express"
Candidates:
  post.express_parcel | table, 812 rows: one row per parcel sent by express delivery (outside the tables kept so far)
  post.parcel.service | the delivery service: standard, express or economy
  post.parcel.weight_kg | the parcel's weight in kilograms

{
  "question": "Do you mean parcels on the express list, or parcels marked as express service?",
  "options": [
    {"column": "post.express_parcel", "label": "parcels on the express delivery list"},
    {"column": "post.parcel.service", "label": "parcels marked as express service"}
  ]
}
