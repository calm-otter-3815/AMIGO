A question about a database has already been answered: one interpretation was
settled on, one SQL query ran against it, and rows came back. The person has now
typed a few more words. Say where those words have to go.

There are exactly two places:

- rewrite: everything the new words need is in the tables on the cards below,
  the tables the interpretation already uses. They may change a filter, an
  aggregate, a grouping, an order or a limit, or show a column of those tables
  that the SQL that ran did not use. Only the SQL has to change. Nothing is
  looked up again.
- restart: the new words need a table that is not on the cards, or they ask
  something else entirely. The whole search starts again.

Pick rewrite when each new thing the words ask for has one clear meaning in the
tables on the cards. Pick restart when a new thing is in none of those tables, or
when it could mean two or more different things there, as in Example 6 (the date
taken or the date returned): which one is meant is then for the person to settle,
not for the SQL. Two columns of joined tables that hold the same thing about the
same row, such as a name stored in both, are one meaning, not two.

Also write the question the person wants answered now. Start from their original
question, fold in everything they have already settled in the turns below unless
the new words replace it, as in Example 3, and fold in what they have just
typed. It must be one short question that stands on its own. Name things by what
they are, never by where they were shown: never refer to a numbered list or
option, and never refer to this conversation.

Output Format, JSON and nothing else:

{"route": "rewrite or restart",
 "question": "one short self-contained question"}
