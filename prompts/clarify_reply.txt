A person is being helped to pin down a question they asked about a database.
Code has already worked out where the possible interpretations disagree, and a
question with a fixed list of options is on the table. Instead of clicking an
option the person typed words of their own. Say what those words do.

They do exactly one of three things:

- They name one of the options on the table. Answer with that option's id and
  leave the question null. Match on meaning, not on spelling: a synonym, a
  shorter form or a paraphrase of a label counts.
- They add something the options never covered, so the question they want
  answered is not quite the one they asked. Answer with a new question and leave
  the option null. Write one short self-contained question that means what they
  want now: start from their original question, fold in everything they have
  already settled in the turns below, and fold in what they have just typed. It
  must stand on its own, differ from the original question, and never point back
  at this conversation.
- They do neither: their words match no option and add nothing usable, or they
  match two options equally well. Answer with both fields null.

The options are fixed. You may not add one, drop one or reword one, and you may
never invent an id. When no option is listed, an option can never be the answer.

Output Format, JSON and nothing else:

{"option": "the id of the option they meant, or null",
 "question": "one short self-contained question, or null"}

Example 1
Original question: Which airports handled the most passengers last year?
Turns so far: none
On the table: Which passengers should be counted?
Options:
  o1 | everyone who passed through
  o2 | domestic flights only
  o3 | people changing planes only
They typed: just the ones flying inside the country

{"option": "o2", "question": null}

Example 2
Original question: Which airports handled the most passengers last year?
Turns so far:
  asked: Which passengers should be counted? | options: everyone who passed through, domestic flights only, people changing planes only | they answered: everyone who passed through
On the table: Which airport name do you mean?
Options:
  o1 | the airport's full name
  o2 | its three-letter code
They typed: actually only the airports in Portugal

{"option": null, "question": "Which airports in Portugal handled the most passengers last year, counting everyone who passed through?"}

Example 3
Original question: List the members who joined the Riverside branch in 2023.
Turns so far: none
On the table: Does Riverside name the branch itself, or the part of town it is in?
Options:
  o1 | the branch called Riverside
  o2 | any branch in the Riverside district
They typed: the one on Mill Lane

{"option": null, "question": null}
