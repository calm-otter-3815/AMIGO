A person asked a question about a data lake: many databases, each with many tables. Code
split the question into parts and built interpretations of it: each one reads every part
from one place (a column), in one database, with its tables joined as shown. Each place
was already judged on its own. Your only job is to say, for each whole interpretation (the
places together, with their joins), yes or no. Code then builds the answer from your
answers; you do not write queries.

- yes: a person asking this question could mean it: a query over exactly these tables,
  joined as shown, reading each part from its place, answers the question, directly or by
  a simple calculation on a place (an age from a birth date).
- no: it answers another question.

How to judge:
- Judge every interpretation on its own. Several can be yes: the same fact kept in two
  tables, or a word with two meanings in this lake. List each of them.
- Look at how the parts combine, not at each place alone:
  - a join relates rows; check that it relates the rows the question relates (the member
    who made the rental, not a member whose home station is the same);
  - a table that holds no part only joins others; check that the question's things are
    related through it, not only by sharing one of its rows;
  - a part read from a place answers the question only when that place, in these tables,
    means what the part means (a date of signing up is not a date of renting).
- Judge only from what is printed below.

Output Format, JSON and nothing else:

{"yes": [numbers of the interpretations a person could mean]}

A number appears at most once. An empty list is allowed.
